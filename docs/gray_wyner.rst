The Gray-Wyner Network
======================

.. note::

   We use :math:`p` to denote fixed probability distributions, and :math:`q` to denote probability distributions that are optimized.

The Gray-Wyner network :cite:`gray1974source` is a simple source-coding network with one encoder and :math:`n` decoders. The encoder observes a correlated source vector :math:`(X_1, \dots, X_n) \sim p` and emits one *common* message at rate :math:`R_0`, delivered to every decoder, together with :math:`n` *private* messages, where message :math:`i` (at rate :math:`R_i`) is delivered only to decoder :math:`i`. Decoder :math:`i` reconstructs :math:`X_i` to within an average distortion :math:`D_i`.

The achievable rate region (lossless: :cite:`gray1974source`; lossy: :cite:`viswanatha2014lossy`) is the set of rate tuples :math:`(R_0, R_1, \dots, R_n)` for which there exists an auxiliary random variable :math:`W` satisfying

.. math::

   R_0 &\ge \I{X_1, \dots, X_n : W} \\
   R_i &\ge R_{X_i \mid W}(D_i) \qquad i = 1, \dots, n

where :math:`R_{X_i \mid W}(D_i)` is the conditional rate-distortion function of :math:`X_i` given :math:`W`. In the lossless case (:math:`D_i = 0` under a Hamming distortion) this reduces to :math:`R_i \ge \H{X_i \mid W}`.

The region is convex, so its lower boundary is traced by minimizing a weighted sum of rates over :math:`W` (and, in the lossy case, the reconstruction test channels subject to the distortion budgets):

.. math::

   \min_{W} \quad \lambda_0 \I{X_{1:n} : W} + \sum_{i} \lambda_i R_{X_i \mid W}(D_i)
   ~.

Sweeping the weights :math:`(\lambda_0, \dots, \lambda_n)` sweeps the Pareto surface.

Common informations as operating points
----------------------------------------

Several of the :doc:`common informations <measures/multivariate/multivariate>` are particular operating points of the lossless Gray-Wyner region:

- The :doc:`measures/multivariate/wyner_common_information` :math:`\C{\cdot}` is the smallest common rate :math:`R_0` on the minimum sum-rate face.
- The :doc:`measures/multivariate/gk_common_information` :math:`\K{\cdot}` is the largest common rate incurring no sum-rate penalty.
- The :doc:`measures/multivariate/exact_common_information` :math:`\G{\cdot}` and :doc:`measures/multivariate/kamath_common_information` :math:`\operatorname{U}(\cdot)` are further extreme points.

``GrayWynerNetwork.corner_points`` returns these values by delegating to their canonical implementations.

Lossy common information
------------------------

Generalizing the minimum-sum-rate operating point to positive distortion gives the lossy common information :math:`C(D_1, \dots, D_n)` :cite:`viswanatha2014lossy`, available as :func:`lossy_wyner_common_information`. For :math:`D_i = 0` it coincides with Wyner's common information.

Other coordinates on the same region
------------------------------------

Several bodies studied independently in the literature turn out to be the lossless Gray-Wyner region written in different coordinates. Each records a different set of affine functions of the entropies of :math:`(X_{0:n}, W)`, but all are generated by the same auxiliary variable.

.. math::

   \tau_i     &= R_0 + R_i - \H{X_i}           &&= \I{X_{-i} : W \mid X_i} \\
   \tau_{res} &= R_0 + \sum_i R_i - \H{X_{0:n}} &&= \T{X_{0:n} \mid W} \\
   v_i        &= \H{X_i} - R_i                 &&= \I{X_i : W} \\
   v_{joint}  &= R_0                           &&= \I{X_{0:n} : W}

The first two identities are the chain rule and the definition of the conditional total correlation. The matrix carrying :math:`(R_0, \dots, R_n)` to :math:`(\tau_0, \dots, \tau_{n-1}, \tau_{res})` is unimodular, so nothing is lost in either direction.

+-------------------------+----------------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
| Body                    | Coordinates                                  | View                             |
+=========================+==============================================+==================================+
| Gray-Wyner region       | :math:`(R_0, R_1, \dots, R_n)`               | ``.region()``                    |
+-------------------------+----------------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
| Region of tension       | :math:`(\tau_0, \dots, \tau_{res})`          | ``.tension_region()``            |
+-------------------------+----------------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
| Extension profile       | :math:`(R_1, \dots, R_n, \tau_{res})`        | ``.extension_profile()``         |
+-------------------------+----------------------------------------------+----------------------------------+
| Mutual information      | :math:`(v_1, \dots, v_n, v_{joint})`         | ``.mutual_information_region()`` |
+-------------------------+----------------------------------------------+----------------------------------+

The region of tension is due to Prabhakaran & Prabhakaran :cite:`prabhakaran2014assisted`; at :math:`n = 2` the residual coordinate is :math:`\I{X : Y \mid W}` and the map is their :math:`g_{X,Y}`. The extension profile is due to Matveev & Romashchenko :cite:`matveev2026beyond`, and the mutual information region to Li & El Gamal :cite:`li2018extended`.

.. warning::

   ``dit`` follows the coordinate ordering of :cite:`prabhakaran2014assisted` and :cite:`li2018extended`, in which coordinate :math:`i` is :math:`\I{X_{-i} : W \mid X_i}` — so for a pair, :math:`(\I{Y : W \mid X}, \I{X : W \mid Y}, \I{X : Y \mid W})`. Csirmaz :cite:`csirmaz2023short` swaps the first two coordinates and omits the increasing hull, correspondingly using a cardinality bound of :math:`|X||Y| + 3` where ``dit`` uses the Fenchel-Eggleston :math:`|X||Y| + 2`.

The region of tension proper is the *increasing hull* of the attainable points; the samplers report attainable points on its lower boundary. Its axis intercepts, ``.tension_intercepts()``, are the :math:`T_i` of :cite:`prabhakaran2014assisted`; the residual intercept satisfies :math:`T_{res} = \I{X : Y} - \K{X : Y}`.

The shape function
------------------

The support function of the extension profile is the *shape function* :cite:`matveev2026beyond`:

.. math::

   S(\alpha) = \sup_W \left( \sum_i \alpha_i \H{X_i \mid W} - \T{X_{0:n} \mid W} \right)
             = \H{X_{0:n}} - \min_W \left( R_0 + \sum_i (1 - \alpha_i) R_i \right)

so it too is a weighted rate query, with weights :math:`(1, 1 - \alpha_1, \dots, 1 - \alpha_n)`. Those are non-negative exactly on the unit cube, which is where the shape function is studied.

Two envelopes depend only on the entropy profile. Evaluating at the deterministic probes :math:`W \in \{X_{0:n}, X_{-j}, \cdot\}` gives the lower one, and convexity together with :math:`S(0) = 0` and :math:`S(e_j) = \H{X_j \mid X_{-j}}` gives the upper one:

.. math::

   S_{\min}(\alpha) &= \max\left\{ 0,\ \max_j \alpha_j \H{X_j \mid X_{-j}},\ \sum_i \alpha_i \H{X_i} - \T{X_{0:n}} \right\} \\
   S_{\max}(\alpha) &= \max\left\{ \sum_j \alpha_j \H{X_j \mid X_{-j}},\ \sum_i \alpha_i \H{X_i} - \T{X_{0:n}} \right\}

A pair attaining the lower envelope everywhere is *rigid*; one attaining the upper envelope has Gács-Körner extractable mutual information. ``ShapeFunction.rigidity()`` reports where a given pair sits between them.

The :doc:`measures/multivariate/tension_common_information` and the entanglement are read off the same region; see that page.

.. note::

   Only the total-correlation residual is affine in the Gray-Wyner rates. Dual-total-correlation and CAEKL variants of the residual coincide with it at :math:`n = 2` but would need their own optimizer for :math:`n \ge 3`, and are not implemented.

Extreme points of the mutual information region
-----------------------------------------------

Li & El Gamal :cite:`li2018extended` identify three quantities as extreme points of the mutual information region, each a maximization of the interaction information :math:`\I{X : Y \mid U} - \I{X : Y}` under a different independence constraint:

.. math::

   \Gnni{X : Y}     &= \max_{U} \I{X : Y \mid U} - \I{X : Y} \\
   \Gpni{X \to Y}   &= \max_{U \ind X} \I{X : Y \mid U} - \I{X : Y} \\
   \Gppi{X : Y}     &= \max_{U \ind X,\, U \ind Y} \I{X : Y \mid U} - \I{X : Y}

They satisfy :math:`0 \le \Gppi{X : Y} \le \Gpni{X \to Y} \le \Gnni{X : Y} \le \min\{\H{X \mid Y}, \H{Y \mid X}\}`. The maximal interaction information vanishes exactly when the support graph contains no path of length three; the symmetric private interaction information vanishes exactly when the support graph is acyclic.

Because these *maximize* rates, they need signed weights, which ``GrayWynerOptimizer`` accepts via ``allow_signed=True``. Signed weights optimize over the raw attainable point set rather than the upward-closed region, over which such a sum would be unbounded.

.. note::

   Unlike the region of tension, which tensorizes exactly, the mutual information region is only superadditive :cite:`li2018extended`.

Spectral diagnostics
--------------------

For a distribution uniform on a *biregular* support, the singular values of the 0/1 biadjacency matrix of the support graph recover part of the entropy profile exactly :cite:`matveev2026spectral`:

.. math::

   \log \lambda_1 = \H{XY} - \tfrac{1}{2}\left( \H{X} + \H{Y} \right),
   \qquad \frac{\lambda_2}{\lambda_1} = \rho_m(X : Y)

where :math:`\rho_m` is the :doc:`maximal correlation <measures/divergences/maximum_correlation>`. Both identities fail off biregular supports. These are available as :func:`~dit.algorithms.support_singular_values`.

The spectral gap bounds the entanglement of a uniform pair from below by :math:`\min\{\I{X : Y},\ 2\log(\lambda_1/\lambda_2)\} - O(\log \H{XY})`, which is what certifies that expander-like supports are maximally non-extractable. :func:`~dit.algorithms.spectral_entanglement_bound` returns the nominal term only.

.. warning::

   The suppressed slop is not a formality. The uniform distribution on an 8-cycle has an entanglement of about :math:`0.67` bits against a nominal bound of :math:`1.00`. Use the nominal term to compare pairs or to reason asymptotically, never as a pointwise certificate.

Example
-------

The trade-off between the common rate and the total private rate is traced by ``GrayWynerCurve``:

.. ipython::
   :verbatim:

   In [1]: from dit.rate_distortion import GrayWynerCurve

   In [2]: d = dit.Distribution(['00', '01', '10', '11'], [0.4, 0.1, 0.1, 0.4])

   In [3]: GrayWynerCurve(d, s_num=21).plot();

The named corner points and the lossy common information:

.. ipython::
   :verbatim:

   In [4]: from dit.rate_distortion import GrayWynerNetwork, lossy_wyner_common_information

   In [5]: from dit.rate_distortion.gray_wyner import hamming_matrix

   In [6]: GrayWynerNetwork(d).corner_points()

   In [7]: dm = [hamming_matrix(2), hamming_matrix(2)]

   In [8]: lossy_wyner_common_information(d, bounds=[0.1, 0.1], distortions=dm)

The tension region and the shape function:

.. ipython::
   :verbatim:

   In [9]: from dit.rate_distortion import ShapeFunction

   In [10]: GrayWynerNetwork(d).tension_intercepts()

   In [11]: ShapeFunction(d, num=21).rigidity()

   In [12]: ShapeFunction(d, num=21).plot();
